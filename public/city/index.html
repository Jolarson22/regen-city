Design principles

From Introduction to Cities (Chen, Orum, Paulsen)
  • Garden city layout. Your plan follows Ebenezer Howard's idea: a green belt round the edge, parks inside, radial boulevards to a central square. Keep the 30% green target inside the ring, not only on it.
  • The plaza is the heart. Public squares are where a city meets and, at times, protests. Keep the Central Plaza open to Icolo e Bengo residents, not only ticket holders.
  • Streets that watch themselves. Jane Jacobs' lesson: mixed uses and people on foot keep streets safe and alive. Put shops, cafés and workshops on the ground floor of the living district, and let crew walk to stages.
  • Mega-venues cost residents. Stadiums built for big events often burden the people around them. Size the Sports & Wellness zone for daily community use first.
  • No enclave. Planned districts can split a city into insiders and outsiders. Free entry, local hiring and shuttle links to Cacuaco and Viana keep RE:GEN part of the province, not a gated island.
  • Nature as infrastructure. The ponds and green spine should carry rainy-season water, which the simulation shows is a real risk.
